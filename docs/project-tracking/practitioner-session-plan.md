# Practitioner Session Plan

## Objective

Use the proposed 90-minute session with Master Aw Yeong Peng Mun to obtain:

- culturally authoritative explanations;
- clear reference demonstrations of the three selected movements;
- practitioner-defined movement phases and acceptable beginner variation;
- tutorial and cultural-insight material;
- technical reference footage suitable for landmark analysis;
- costume and safety guidance.

The session should prioritize irreplaceable practitioner knowledge. Camera calibration,
file organization, and internal team discussion must happen outside the teacher's
allotted time.

## Before the session

Confirm with the organisers:

- exact location, arrival instructions, and contact person;
- permission to record video and audio;
- permitted use for development, model evaluation or training, public tutorial
  footage, promotion, and portfolio documentation;
- whether any uses must remain private;
- release-form requirements;
- whether another performer or student will demonstrate;
- whether Dan costume and water sleeves will be available;
- room dimensions, lighting, background, ambient noise, and power access;
- whether the team may arrive at least 30 minutes early.

Prepare:

- primary camera capable of at least 1080p/30 fps; 60 fps if reliable;
- tripod at approximately exhibition-camera height;
- second camera or phone for a 45-degree reference angle;
- laptop with the provisional exhibition-camera view;
- duplicate storage;
- power cables, batteries, and extension lead;
- microphone if available;
- floor markers and measuring tape;
- consent or release paperwork;
- printed shot list and practitioner questions.

Do not rely only on the laptop webcam for archival reference. Still capture the
exhibition-webcam view because it represents what the detector will see.

## Proposed 90-minute schedule

| Time | Activity |
|---:|---|
| 0–5 min | Introductions, purpose, consent, and intended uses |
| 5–15 min | Dan-role context, movement vocabulary, costume, and story guidance |
| 15–30 min | Lotus Finger explanation and capture |
| 30–47 min | Door-opening action explanation and capture |
| 47–67 min | Water-sleeve explanation, safety, and capture |
| 67–77 min | Beginner variations, mirroring, and common mistakes |
| 77–84 min | Short tutorial phrases and cultural-insight wording |
| 84–90 min | Playback check, missing shots, final questions, and approval process |

If time slips, prioritize cultural explanation, clean front demonstrations, and
essential-versus-optional features. Promotional footage is lower priority.

## Capture protocol for each movement

Record separate clips rather than one continuous performance:

1. Practitioner states the movement name.
2. Practitioner explains its meaning and dramatic use.
3. Slow demonstration with verbal phase-by-phase explanation.
4. Normal performance-speed demonstration.
5. Three to five clean repetitions from the front.
6. Two repetitions from approximately 45 degrees.
7. Start pose held for two seconds.
8. Each important transition held briefly.
9. Final pose held for two seconds.
10. Acceptable simplified beginner version.
11. Example of a variation that should still count.
12. Example of a similar movement that changes the meaning, if appropriate.

For Lotus Finger, obtain close reference footage of the hand while retaining a full
upper-body take for posture and arm position.

For the door-opening action, establish whether it is one gesture, a left/right
sequence, or a short temporal action with multiple phases.

For water sleeves, capture:

- with the intended sleeve garment;
- without sleeves so the underlying arm mechanics are visible;
- at normal and reduced speed;
- the minimum safe movement range;
- fabric behavior from front and 45-degree views.

## Camera framing

The primary technical view should approximate the exhibition system:

- landscape;
- head, shoulders, torso, full arms, hands, and sleeve extent visible;
- camera approximately at chest-to-shoulder height unless practitioner advice differs;
- performer roughly 1–2 metres away;
- static tripod;
- neutral, contrasting background where possible;
- no portrait, cinematic, or artificial background effects;
- fixed exposure and focus if reliable.

Capture a calibration reference showing the performer's position and measured camera
distance.

## File and metadata convention

Use:

```text
2026-08-01_<gesture>_<view>_<speed>_<take>.<extension>
```

Examples:

```text
2026-08-01_lotus-finger_front_slow_take-01.mp4
2026-08-01_door-opening_front_normal_take-03.mp4
2026-08-01_water-sleeves_45deg_normal_take-02.mp4
```

Maintain a shot log containing filename, gesture, view, distance, costume state,
speed, practitioner notes, cultural approval, technical quality, and permitted uses.
Make two verified copies. Raw footage must not be committed to the public repository.

## Privacy, consent, and rights

Document:

- who is being recorded;
- who owns the footage;
- where it may be stored;
- whether it may appear in the public game;
- whether it may be used for model evaluation or training;
- whether it may be shown in portfolios or presentations;
- retention and deletion expectations;
- attribution requirements;
- who may approve edited derivatives.

Permission to film should not be assumed to include model training, public display, or
promotion. Obtain explicit terms for each intended use.

## After the session

Within 24 hours:

1. Verify and duplicate all files.
2. Create a read-only raw-footage archive.
3. Complete the shot log.
4. Transcribe cultural explanations.
5. Extract candidate movement phases and technical features.
6. Send uncertain names, translations, and interpretations for review.
7. Create landmark-only development fixtures when M2 is available.
8. Do not train a model until dataset need, consent, and evaluation criteria are
   explicitly established.

## Success criteria

For all three movements, obtain:

- a culturally reviewed name and meaning;
- clean front reference at slow and normal speed;
- identified movement phases;
- essential and optional features;
- acceptable beginner simplification;
- mirroring guidance;
- tutorial emphasis;
- cultural-insight wording;
- footage-use permissions;
- water-sleeve safety and camera-visibility guidance.
